## Scope and limitations

1. The free-space dipole is 10 m at 14.2 MHz, about 0.474 wavelengths, not *exactly* a half-wave dipole. Its impedance must not be compared as an exact match to the ideal `73 + j42.5 ohm` half-wave value.
2. NEC2 `GN 2` and PyMININEC `--medium` use different ground representations. Matching the numerical permittivity/conductivity does not establish identical solutions. The very large ground-case disagreement requires further investigation before it is attributed to either solver.
3. The vertical radial junction contains five wires. NEC2 excites the center of the first wire segment, whereas PyMININEC excites a pulse. This is not a strictly equivalent feed. Report this as a *topology/feed sensitivity diagnostic*, not a formal intersolver validation.
4. Doubling the number of segments is a preliminary refinement check, **not** proof of asymptotic convergence. A third density, feed-convergence checks, and sensitivity to wire radius/segmentation are recommended.
5. The existing `bench` run does not request comparable far-field grids for both engines. **Maximum gain, radiation pattern error and realized gain were not validated** in this study. The new MININEC v1.1.0 postprocessor can export these where available, but a controlled comparison needs explicit NEC2 `RP` cards, matched angles and consistent gain definition/normalization.
6. All claims above are about these specific model setups and pinned executable versions, not the general superiority or accuracy of a solver.

## Findings

The dipole in free space provides a satisfactory first cross-engine consistency check at the impedance level. The two soil-sensitive cases exhibit differences much greater than their respective two-mesh changes, underscoring a model-equivalence problem rather than proving an algorithmic failure. A next-stage formal validation should isolate ground modeling and feed geometry before comparing far-field and gain quantities.
